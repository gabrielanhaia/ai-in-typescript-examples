# Staff handbook — 4. The returns desk

Last updated: 6 January 2026
Applies to: goods-in unit, Marbury Trading Estate

This is the internal procedure. The customer-facing rules are in the returns
and refunds policy, and where the two disagree, the policy is what the customer
was promised and the policy wins.

## The window

Customers get thirty days to send something back. **The 30-day window runs from
the delivery date recorded by the carrier, not from the order date and not from
the date the customer says it arrived.**

That distinction is the whole reason this paragraph exists. A customer who
ordered on the 1st, received on the 6th, and posted the return on the 4th of
the following month is inside the window even though it is thirty-four days
after the order. Check the tracking before declining anything on time.

Where the carrier has no delivery date — an untracked item under 2 kg — take
the customer's word for it. We have never once found this to be abused in a way
worth the argument.

## Receiving

1. Scan the return label. If there is no label, the parcel goes on the
   unmatched shelf and Customer Operations is emailed the same day with a photo
   of the outside of the box.
2. Open over the bench, not on the floor. Small parts fall out of returns.
3. Photograph the item before touching it, every time, no exceptions. The photo
   is the evidence in every dispute that follows.
4. Check the item against the order line. A different part number in the box is
   a swap and goes straight to a team lead.

## Grading

Three outcomes, and the grader picks one.

**A — resaleable.** Unfitted, complete, original packaging, nothing missing.
Back to stock, refund authorised.

**B — resaleable with work.** Correct and unfitted, but the packaging is
damaged or the instruction sheet is missing. Repack from the spares shelf,
back to stock, refund authorised.

**C — not resaleable.** Fitted, marked, incomplete, or a consumable that has
been used. Refund is not authorised at the desk. Photograph it, write the
reason, and pass it to Customer Operations, who will speak to the customer.

The C decision is the one that gets appealed, so the written reason has to name
what you saw: "bead marks on both sidewalls", not "looks used".

## The fitted test

Bolted on, even briefly, counts as fitted. Cassettes mark the splines. Chains
show on the rollers. Tyres show bead marks. Frames show a pressed bottom
bracket. If you cannot tell, say so on the note rather than guessing in either
direction.

Helmets: try-on is fine, a scuffed shell or a compressed liner is not.

## Refunds

The desk authorises. Finance issues. The policy commits us to five working days
from arrival, and in practice the desk should clear a return the same day it is
graded, which leaves the whole margin for the days finance needs.

Delivery charges: refund the outbound standard charge when the whole order came
back, not on a partial return, and never the next-day premium.

## Faulty returns

A faulty item is not a return, it is a warranty claim. Do not grade it, do not
authorise a refund, and do not put it back into stock. It goes on the warranty
shelf with the job number written on the bag, and the workshop inspects it.

The single most common error at this desk is grading a faulty item as C — not
resaleable — and starting an argument with a customer who was entitled to a
replacement all along.

## Unmatched and abandoned returns

Parcels with no label or no traceable order sit on the unmatched shelf for
ninety days. Photograph, log, and email Customer Operations weekly with the
list. After ninety days they are disposed of or donated, and the log is what
proves we tried.

## Restocking charges

Retail customers are never charged a restocking fee. Trade accounts are, on
non-stock items only, at the rate in the trade account terms. The desk does not
apply it — it is applied by Finance against the trade account, and the desk's
job is only to mark the return as a non-stock line.
