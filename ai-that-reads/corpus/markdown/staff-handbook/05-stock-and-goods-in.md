# Staff handbook — 5. Stock and goods in

Last updated: 6 January 2026
Applies to: goods-in unit, Marbury Trading Estate

## Receiving a delivery

1. Count the boxes against the carrier's manifest before signing. Sign for the
   number of boxes, never "unchecked".
2. Photograph any box that is crushed, wet or re-taped, before opening it.
3. Book the delivery note against the purchase order in the stock system the
   same day. A delivery note left on the desk overnight is a stock discrepancy
   the following week.
4. Discrepancies — short, over, wrong part, damaged — go to Buying the same
   day with the delivery note number and the photographs.

## Putting away

Everything goes to its bin location. There is no "temporarily on the floor by
the racking", because that is where stock goes to die.

Part numbers are checked against the bin label, not against what the box looks
like. Halvard adapters in particular are visually near-identical between
generations, and the difference between one adapter and its successor is bolt
length and offset, not appearance.

Where a part supersedes another, the old bin is emptied to the clearance shelf
and the label changed the same day. The superseded parts register is updated by
Buying, not by goods in, but goods in is usually who notices first.

## Picking

Pick by the pick list, in bin order. The list is built from the pick queue, and
the pick queue is ordered by payment time.

Scan every line. A line picked without a scan is a line that will be short at
the next count.

If a bin is empty and the system says it is not, do not substitute, do not pick
a similar part, and do not leave it. Mark the line short, finish the rest of the
order, and tell the supervisor. A short line becomes a customer email within the
hour; a substituted line becomes a return in a fortnight.

## Packing

- Small parts in the padded envelope, one order per envelope.
- Anything with a sharp edge — rotors, chainrings, cut cable — gets a card
  sleeve, because it will otherwise come through the envelope in the van.
- Wheels in the wheel box with the axle removed and taped to the hub.
- Framesets in the frame box with the fork separated and the dropouts blocked.
- Never post a hydraulic hose coiled tighter than the loop on the shelf card.
- Every parcel gets the dispatch note. The dispatch note is not a VAT invoice
  and does not carry prices.

## Weight and shipping band

The system prices delivery from the basket weight, in the three bands in the
shipping policy. Goods in does not override the band. If a parcel is genuinely
heavier than the system thinks, tell Fulfilment and let them correct the
product weight, because the same error will repeat on every future order.

## Stock counts

Rolling counts: one aisle a week, on Wednesdays, before picking starts. Full
count once a year, on 14 August, when the workshop closes for stock-taking.

A count discrepancy is recorded, not corrected on the spot. Buying reviews the
week's discrepancies and adjusts. A picker who adjusts stock to make a count
balance has hidden the problem rather than fixed it.

## Damaged and returned stock

Stock damaged in the unit goes on the write-off shelf with a note naming what
happened. There is no penalty for dropping something; there is a real problem
with a part that quietly goes back on the shelf with a dented box.

Returns arrive at the same building but are not goods-in stock until the
returns desk has graded them. Do not put a return away.

## Hazardous lines

Sealant, aerosol degreaser and CO2 cartridges are stored in the cage by the
roller door, not on the general racking. CO2 and sealed pressurised items
cannot travel by air, which is why those lines are UK and Republic of Ireland
only. The system enforces it; goods in should still notice if one is picked for
an EU order, because that is a system error worth reporting.

## Waste

Cardboard flattened into the baler. Plastic film into the clear sacks. Battery
packs into the steel bin in the yard, never in general waste, never in the
baler.
