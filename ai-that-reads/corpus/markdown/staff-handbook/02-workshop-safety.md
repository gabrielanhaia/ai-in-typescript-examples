# Staff handbook — 2. Workshop safety

Last updated: 6 January 2026
Applies to: Manchester workshop

This section is not advisory. Every rule in it exists because something went
wrong somewhere, and most of them are things you will be tempted to skip on a
busy Saturday morning.

## Personal protective equipment

- Safety glasses whenever a tool is turned on, whenever a spoke is cut, and
  whenever brake fluid is open. Not "when you remember" — the cutter and the
  bleed kit both throw things at eye height.
- Nitrile gloves for any fluid work. Mineral oil is not friendly and DOT fluid
  is worse.
- No loose sleeves, no lanyards, no rings near the truing stand or the bench
  grinder.
- Ear protection on the compressor when the tank is filling.

PPE is stocked in the cupboard by the parts washer. If a box is empty, tell the
manager and take a box from the goods-in unit; do not work without it and do
not wait until tomorrow.

## Brake fluid

DOT fluid strips paint, damages some plastics, and is a skin irritant. Mineral
oil is milder and is still not something to get on your hands.

- Bleed over the tray, never over the bench.
- Decant into the labelled bottle, never into an unlabelled one.
- Waste fluid into the drum by the back door, which is collected quarterly.
  Never down the sink. The unit is on a combined drain and we would be
  prosecuted.
- Any spill gets absorbed with granules from the yellow bin and bagged as
  hazardous waste, not wiped with a rag and binned.

## Solvents and degreasers

The parts washer runs a water-based solution and is safe with gloves. Aerosol
degreaser is not — it is used outside the roller door or under the extraction,
never in the middle of the workshop, and never near an open flame or the bench
grinder.

## Compressed air

The line is set to 8 bar. It is for tyres, for blowing out a bearing seat, and
for nothing else. Do not use it to clean clothing, do not point it at another
person, and do not use it to dry your hands. The tank is drained at opening and
at closing because water in the tank corrodes it from the inside.

## Bikes on the stand

A bike goes on the stand clamped at the seatpost, never at a carbon frame tube.
Where the seatpost is carbon, use the dummy post from the drawer. A bike that
cannot be clamped safely goes in the wheel-on stand on the floor instead.

Nothing is left on a stand at closing. A bike that is halfway through a job
comes off the stand and goes on the rack with its parts bagged and labelled.

## Electric bikes

Batteries come off the bike and go in the steel cabinet before any work starts,
and they go back on at handover. A battery that is swollen, dented, has been in
a crash, or has been in water does not go in the cabinet and does not come into
the workshop at all — it goes in the steel bin in the yard and the manager is
told immediately.

We do not open battery packs. We do not repair chargers. We do not accept a
bike with a battery from a manufacturer we cannot identify.

## Lifting

Boxed complete bikes and boxed wheelsets are a two-person lift from the van.
The goods-in unit has a trolley; the workshop has one too and it lives by the
roller door. Nobody is being judged for asking for a hand.

## Accidents and near misses

Anything that drew blood, anything that went in an eye, and anything that could
have done either goes in the accident book by the door on the day it happened.
The near-miss half of that sentence is the important half. A rag that caught in
the grinder and did not take a hand with it is a near miss and it gets written
down.

The first-aid kit is by the accident book. The nearest walk-in centre and the
nearest eye casualty are both listed on the card inside the lid.

## Fire

Extinguishers: CO2 by the bench, foam by the roller door. The brake fluid drum
and the aerosol shelf are both in the fire plan and both are why we do not
store anything else in that corner.

On the alarm, everybody leaves through the roller door, nobody goes back for a
bike, and the assembly point is the far side of the yard by the fence.
