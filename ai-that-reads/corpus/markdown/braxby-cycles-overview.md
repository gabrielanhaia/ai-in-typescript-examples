# Braxby Cycles — company overview

An internal summary, written for new staff and for anybody who has to describe
the business to somebody outside it. It is not marketing copy and it should not
be pasted into any.

## What we are

Braxby Cycles is an online retailer of bicycle parts, trading at
`www.braxbycycles.example` since 2014. We sell components, wheels, framesets,
tyres and accessories, and we run one physical workshop.

We are a parts business first. Complete bikes are a small share of revenue and
exist mostly because a customer who has bought parts from us for three years
eventually wants a whole bike from the same people.

## What we sell

Six house ranges account for most of what we ship.

- **Halvard** — hydraulic disc brakes, calipers, levers, rotors and adapters.
  Part numbers begin `BRK-`.
- **Norbury** — drivetrain. Chains, cassettes, chainrings, derailleurs. Part
  numbers begin `DRV-`.
- **Emberly** — wheels and hubs. Part numbers begin `WHL-`.
- **Wickhaven** — frames and rigid forks. Part numbers begin `FRM-`.
- **Draycott** — tyres and tubes. Part numbers begin `TYR-`.
- **Pellow** — lights and accessories. Part numbers begin `ACC-`.

Adapters and small fitting parts that are not range-specific carry `ADP-`
numbers. Everything else in the catalogue is a third-party line and carries the
manufacturer's own part number.

## Where we are

One workshop, in Manchester, with a trade counter attached. One goods-in and
returns unit at Marbury Trading Estate, Manchester, which is where every order
is picked and every return is received. The two sites are separate buildings
and post sent to the wrong one adds three days.

There is no shop floor. Customers come to the workshop to collect orders and to
book service work, and there is nothing to browse when they get there.

## How the business is organised

Four teams.

**Customer Operations** owns the support inbox, returns, warranty decisions and
every customer-facing policy document.

**Fulfilment** owns goods-in, picking, packing, dispatch and the carrier
relationships. Fulfilment also owns the shipping policy.

**The Manchester workshop** owns service work, wheel building, complete-bike
builds, technical bulletins and the technical half of any warranty claim.

**Buying** owns the range, supplier relationships, the superseded parts
register and stock levels.

## How we talk to customers

Plainly, and without inventing an answer. The house rule that matters most is
that a member of staff who does not know says so and finds out, rather than
producing something plausible. The customer conversations section of the staff
handbook covers this properly.

We do not quote prices in support conversations. The product page is the price
and it is the only price, because it is the one that updates.

## Seasonality

March to September is roughly two-thirds of the year's volume. The workshop
books out two weeks ahead from April, and the support inbox roughly doubles.
The quiet months are November to January, which is when policy documents get
rewritten and when the range for the next season is bought.

The workshop closes for one day a year, on 14 August, for stock-taking.
