# Workshop procedure — pre-delivery inspection

Reference: WKS-PRO-004
Last updated: 15 January 2026
Applies to: Manchester workshop

Every complete bike leaving the workshop gets a pre-delivery inspection, and
every pre-delivery inspection is signed. This applies to a bike we built from a
frameset, a bike that arrived boxed from a supplier, and a bike that came back
from a warranty repair.

A bike that has had a pre-delivery inspection is a workshop-built assembly for
warranty purposes, whether or not we laced a single spoke. The warranty policy
sets the term for that category; this document sets what has to be true before
the sticker goes on.

## The checks, in order

**1. Frame and fork.** Look for shipping damage, paint cracks at the head tube
and the down tube, and any mark that suggests the box was dropped on a corner.
Check the derailleur hanger alignment before anything else is judged, because a
bent hanger will make a perfectly good drivetrain look wrong.

**2. Headset.** Preload set, no play with the front brake on and the bike
rocked, no notchiness through the rotation. Top cap torque to the frame
manufacturer's figure, and stem bolts after.

**3. Wheels.** True, tension and dish to the wheel building standard, even on a
factory wheel. Factory wheels routinely arrive out of tolerance and it is
cheaper to correct it on the stand than to have it come back at 300 km.

**4. Tyres.** Correct rotation, bead seated evenly all the way round on both
sides, pressure set to the middle of the recommended range and the figure
written on the handover sheet.

**5. Brakes.** Rotors true, no rub through a full rotation, pads bedded on the
bench roller or on the test ride. Lever reach set. Hose routing checked for a
kink at the bar. Every mount bolt torqued to figure.

**6. Drivetrain.** Shifting checked through every gear in both directions,
under a little load rather than free-spinning. Chain length checked against the
largest sprocket and the largest ring. Chainring bolts torqued.

**7. Contact points.** Saddle level and clamp torqued. Bar and stem torqued to
figure, with assembly compound on carbon. Bar tape finished properly, because
it is the first thing a customer touches and the first thing they judge.

**8. Fasteners.** Every fastener on the bike, to figure. There is no "obviously
fine" fastener on this list.

**9. Accessories.** Anything the customer ordered fitted — bottle cages, lights,
mudguards, a computer mount — fitted, torqued and working.

## The test ride

Every bike is ridden. Not rolled across the workshop floor: ridden, outside,
for at least five minutes, including a hard stop from speed and a shift under
load in both directions.

The test ride is where a bike that passes every static check reveals a rubbing
rotor, a creaking bottom bracket or a headset that was preloaded on a stand and
is not preloaded on the road.

If the weather makes a road test unsafe, the bike waits. It does not go out
untested.

## Signing off

The inspector signs the sheet with their name, not their initials, and the
sheet goes in the job record. The sheet lists the torque figures used, the tyre
pressures set, and anything that was corrected during the inspection.

The correction list is the part people skip and the part that is worth
something later. A factory wheel that needed 40 minutes of truing is a supplier
quality problem, and Buying can only act on it if somebody wrote it down.

## Handover

Talk the customer through: the tyre pressures, the first-service interval, the
300 km wheel re-tension, and the brake bedding-in if the pads are new. Hand over
the torque figures for anything they are likely to adjust themselves.

Book the first service before they leave if they are willing. A bike whose
first service is booked comes back; a bike whose owner was told to "give us a
ring in six weeks" usually does not.
