# Warranty policy

This policy sets out what Braxby Cycles covers, for how long, and what a customer
has to do to make a claim. It applies to everything sold through
`www.braxbycycles.example` and to work carried out at the Manchester workshop. It
does not replace a customer's statutory rights, and nothing in it should be read
as limiting them.

Customer Operations owns this document. The workshop owns the technical
judgement inside a claim; Customer Operations owns the decision.

## 1. Definitions

These terms are used consistently throughout the rest of the policy, and the
categories in section 6 depend on them.

**Component.** Any item sold as a single part or as a boxed set of parts, in the
condition it left our supplier. A caliper, a cassette, a wheelset in its factory
packaging, a frameset in its factory packaging.

**Consumable.** A part designed to wear out in normal use. The list is closed
and appears in section 4.

**Workshop-built assembly.** Anything the Manchester workshop has assembled,
laced, tensioned, bled, faced or aligned before it left us. A wheel laced and
tensioned by the workshop is a workshop-built assembly, whether or not the rim
and the hub were bought from Braxby Cycles. A complete bike built to order in
the workshop is a workshop-built assembly. A wheelset that arrived boxed from
the supplier and was shipped on unopened is not — that is a component.

**Bare frame.** A frame or frameset sold on its own, with or without a fork,
with no drivetrain, wheels or contact points fitted.

**Complete bike.** A bicycle sold ready to ride, whether built in the workshop
or shipped boxed from a supplier.

## 2. What is covered

Braxby Cycles covers manufacturing defects and defects in materials. In
practice that means a part that fails in normal use without having been
crashed, modified, corroded through neglect, or fitted outside the
manufacturer's stated limits.

A claim needs three things: the order number, a description of how the part
failed, and photographs. The workshop will ask for the part itself in most
cases, and Customer Operations will send a prepaid label when it does.

## 3. What is not covered

- Damage from a crash, an impact, or a fall.
- Damage from a fitting error, whether by the customer or by a third-party
  mechanic. Work carried out in our own workshop is covered by section 7.
- Corrosion, seizure or wear caused by a lack of maintenance. The maintenance
  we expect is the schedule in the service intervals document.
- Cosmetic marks, paint chips, and finish wear that do not affect function.
- Any part that has been modified, re-drilled, re-faced by a third party, or
  refinished.
- Loss of performance that is ordinary wear. A brake pad that is thin, a chain
  that has stretched, or a tyre that has worn through its tread indicator is a
  consumable at end of life, not a defect.

## 4. Consumables

The following are consumables and carry no warranty term beyond the point of
delivery, other than for a defect present when the part arrived:

brake pads; brake and gear cables and their outers; tyres; inner tubes;
tubeless sealant; chains; cassettes; chainrings; jockey wheels; bar tape;
grips; saddle covers; bearings and bearing seals; bottom bracket cups;
disc rotors once bedded in.

A consumable that arrives visibly damaged or fails on its first ride is handled
under the returns policy, not under this one, and the customer should not wait
for a warranty decision before saying so.

## 5. Crash replacement

Crash replacement is a discretionary scheme, separate from warranty, and it
exists because a frame that has been crashed is not a defective frame.

**Bare frames purchased from Braxby Cycles are covered by the crash-replacement
scheme.** A customer who crashes a bare frame bought from us within thirty-six
months of the order date can buy a replacement frame of the same model, or the
nearest current equivalent, at forty per cent off the published price.

**Frames supplied as part of a complete bike are not covered by the
crash-replacement scheme.** The frame in a complete bike is not sold as a
separate line, so there is no frame order to date the scheme from, and the
discount is not offered. This has caught customers out and it is worth saying
plainly at the point of sale.

The scheme is discretionary. The workshop inspects the damage, confirms it is
consistent with an impact rather than with a defect, and Customer Operations
approves or declines. A declined crash-replacement request does not prevent a
warranty claim on the same frame if the workshop's inspection suggests one.

## 6. Term by category

The term runs from the date the order was delivered, not from the date it was
placed, and not from the date the customer first rode it.

| Category | Term | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Components | 24 months | The default. Everything not named below. |
| Wickhaven frames and rigid forks | 60 months | Original purchaser only, non-transferable. |
| Workshop-built assemblies | 36 months | Covers the build itself: spoke tension, bearing preload, hose routing, facing and alignment. |
| Electronics and lighting | 24 months | Batteries are covered against defect, not against capacity loss. |
| Consumables | none | See section 4. |
| Trade account orders | 12 months | Trade terms override this table; see the trade account terms. |

A workshop-built assembly that fails because of a defective component is
handled as a component claim, on the component's term. The two terms sit
alongside each other and the customer gets the benefit of whichever is longer.

## 7. Work carried out in our workshop

Labour carried out by the Manchester workshop is guaranteed for twelve months.
If a bleed will not hold, a wheel goes out of true under normal use, or a
bearing preload backs off, bring it back and we redo the work at no charge.

There is one exception, and it is absolute: **where the customer supplied the
part, the labour guarantee does not apply.** The workshop cannot stand behind a
build using a component it did not sell and cannot inspect the provenance of.
The workshop service terms set out which customer-supplied parts we will fit at
all.

## 8. Making a claim

1. Email `support@braxbycycles.example` with the order number and photographs.
2. Customer Operations acknowledges within one working day and either resolves
   it or raises a workshop inspection.
3. Where the part is needed, a prepaid label is issued and the part is sent to
   the returns unit at Marbury Trading Estate, Manchester.
4. A decision is issued within ten working days of the part arriving.
5. An approved claim is settled by replacement where we hold stock, by credit
   where we do not, and by refund where neither is possible.

We do not charge an inspection fee, and we do not charge return carriage on a
claim that is approved. A claim that is declined and where the customer wants
the part back is returned at our cost once; after that we hold the part for
sixty days and then dispose of it.
