# Recommended service intervals

These are the intervals the Manchester workshop works to. They are what we mean
when the warranty policy refers to "the maintenance we expect". They are
deliberately conservative: a rider who follows them will occasionally service
something that did not need it yet, and will almost never be stranded by
something that did.

Two riders on the same bike will not wear it at the same rate. Distance is the
better trigger where we have given one; time is the fallback where wear is
driven by contamination and corrosion rather than by use.

## Brakes

**Hydraulic disc brakes should be bled every 12 months.** Mineral oil and DOT
fluid both take on water over time, and a lever that has gone slightly long
over a season is usually a fluid problem rather than a pad problem.

**Hydraulic disc brakes used for wet-weather commuting should be bled every 6
months.** Year-round commuting, winter road riding and regular wet gravel all
count. The mechanism is the same; the rate is roughly double.

Pads are inspected every 500 km and replaced at 1 mm of friction material
remaining above the backing plate. Rotors are replaced at 1.5 mm thickness.

Rim brakes: pads inspected every 500 km, cables and outers replaced every 12
months, and the braking surface checked against the manufacturer's wear
indicator at every service.

## Drivetrain

| Task | Interval |
|---|---|
| Chain wear check | Every 1,000 km |
| Chain replacement | At 0.5% elongation on an 11- or 12-speed chain |
| Cassette replacement | Typically every third chain |
| Chainring inspection | Every 3,000 km |
| Gear cable and outer replacement | Every 12 months, or every 6 months for winter use |
| Derailleur hanger alignment check | At every service and after any fall |

A chain replaced on time is the cheapest maintenance there is. A chain replaced
late takes the cassette with it, and often the chainrings after that.

## Wheels

Wheel true and tension check every 2,000 km. A new wheel or a workshop-built
wheel should come back at 300 km for a first re-tension; spokes bed in and the
first check is not optional if the warranty on the build is to hold.

Hub bearings: inspected every 5,000 km, serviced or replaced when there is
detectable play or roughness. Sealed cartridge bearings are replaced rather
than serviced.

Tubeless sealant is topped up every 3 months and replaced entirely every 6
months. A tyre that has been standing unridden for a season will usually need
the sealant scraped out rather than topped up.

## Frame and contact points

Headset service every 12 months. **Headsets on bikes ridden through the winter
should be serviced every 6 months**, because the failure is corrosion in the
lower bearing race and it is driven by road salt rather than by distance.

Bottom bracket inspected every 5,000 km. Seatpost removed, cleaned and
regreased every 12 months — on carbon frames, with assembly compound rather
than grease, and to the frame manufacturer's torque figure.

Bolt torque check across the whole bike every 6 months, and always after
travelling with the bike disassembled.

## What a full service covers

The workshop's full service is a fixed list, and it is on the workshop price
list. It includes a bleed, a drivetrain clean and measure, a wheel true and
tension, a headset and bottom bracket check, a bolt torque check, and a gear
and brake set-up. It does not include parts, and it does not include a wheel
rebuild.

## After a crash

Anything that has hit the ground hard comes in for an inspection before it goes
back out, whatever the intervals above say. The specific checks are the
derailleur hanger, the bar and stem for creases and marks, the frame at the
head tube and the down tube, and the wheels for a flat spot. Carbon components
that have taken an impact are replaced rather than inspected and passed.
